# Vision & philosophical foundation

## Core proposition

> Power is temporary. Identity and the will to reclaim home can outlive conquest.

Conquest is a long-horizon *living simulation* in which the player's strategic judgment matters more than menus. The aesthetic and mechanical identity are linked: colored flags remain on national homelands, even while their current owners change.

The game has no ordinary YOU LOST / GAME OVER screen. If a faction loses every sector, the homeland continues producing resistance recruits. The player decides when to take a risk, attack the occupier, and rebuild. Rival AI states face the same system.

## Four design pillars

1. **Immediate fun:** Select, send, watch the front move. Direct manipulation before abstract interfaces.
2. **Consequential geography:** Every border is a decision: attack, reinforce, absorb, or hold reserves.
3. **Defeat is a turning point:** No permanent death or forced restart. A comeback is earned through timing.
4. **Simplicity with emergent strategy:** Small number of clearly explained rules; many plausible outcomes.

## Future depth — not yet a promise

Optional historical scenarios, factions, cultural identity, smarter political behavior, campaigns and credible multiplayer may be explored. They are not permission to bury the moment-to-moment game under UI.

## Success metric

During a five-minute playtest, a newcomer should take territory in under 20 seconds, understand why one battle was won or lost, make a deliberate choice between expansion and defense, and feel that a lost homeland creates a new goal rather than a dead end.
